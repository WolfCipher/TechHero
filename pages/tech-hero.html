Catt Small

Catt Small is a video game developer and product designer living in New York. She first began programming when she was 10, in order to create her own dress-up dolls. This reflects her current career straddling the boundaries of code and art, as well as her studies in Graphic Design and Integrated Digital Media. Small has contributed to projects building websites and video games, through a wide variety of avenues. Beyond contributing to the code itself, Small also contributed through design and UI/UX.

Catt Small is an avid leader, sharing her knowledge with many learners. She organized monthly events for newbies in design and technology for about three years. Small even made contributions to minorities in a few ways. For example, Small helped to create and run Code Liberation, a non-profit organization focused on teaching gender minorities how to program video games. Additionally, Small helps organize events for Game Devs of Color Expo, which provides a platform for people of color to showcase their skills and projects. Furthermore, Small helped create Good for PoC, which helped people of color find supportive work environments in tech for two years, before donating its remaining funds to help give people of color opportunities in tech.

Catt Small is proof that a typical trajectory is not necessary for success. Her personal timeline documents numerous ways she has contributed to various projects, whether for the simple joy of improving user experience or for the pleasure of making the world a better place. In fact, her impact may be more far-reaching than the work of employees at big tech companies. That's what makes her so inspiring. Most coders aren't headed for big tech, so seeing someone like Small create real change matters. It gives the rest of us hope that we can matter, too.

The CyberCode Twins

The CyberCode Twins, America and Penelope Lopez, are first-generation Latina twin sisters born and raised in Los Angeles, California. They have made a name for themselves by developing technology for social justice. They are also known for earning multiple awards in tech competitions and hackathons. They have been at the White House and at the United Nations.

In the Lopez twins' TEDxLAPL talk The Hackathon Effect, they discuss their unconventional path to the computer science field. Their family had limited access to technology. They began coding in college as one of the few students with no prior coding experience. But at their first hackathon, an event where programmers and non-programmers spend 24 hours learning how to develop an app, they found opportunities—to improve society and start a career. This pushed them to attend multiple hackathons and competitions.

The issues that Penelope and America Lopez target with their technology are remarkably varied. Their VR project Sobriety Lab was designed to reduce barriers that victims of substance abuse (especially those of Hispanic heritage) face when getting help, such as stigma and language barriers. Their project SnapFood provided online access to supermarkets accepting food stamps, allowing users to access food without traveling long distances, and was inspired by personal experiences. Their project Beacon of Hope aimed to help victims of trafficking access with smart bathroom dispensaries.

Recently, the CyberCode Twins have been exploring blockchain and ways to support others in their tech career. They partnered with Toyota Latino and Remezcla to give two-on-one advice to emerging Latino entrepreneurs hoping to work with blockchain technology or hoping to develop a technology for social change. As Penelope Lopez says in reference to blockchain, "since this space is so early, Latinas have a chance to get into this space."
