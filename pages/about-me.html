Fun Facts About (Identical) Twins

Biology

  • How likely a couple is to have fraternal twins depends on the mother's DNA. Hyper-ovulating women are more likely to conceive fraternal twins. The cause of identical twins is still unknown!
  • Identical twins can have different allergies! This boils down to gene expression. Even with the same DNA, epigenetic factors (e.g. the environment) can affect your traits / gene expression. (Note that DNA mutation rates means identical twins don't necessarily have identical DNA. This fact is a nice excuse to ask people to call us 'twins' instead of 'clones.')

On a different note, this course got us to ponder ways twins may be included/excluded by the system.

  • Lost Applications
    • Whether through human or machine error (not sure which), only one of us tends to get a response on applications.
    • Example: When applying for the honors section of a course around the same time using Google forms, only one of us got admitted. An email to the professor revealed that the other application went missing!
  • Birthdate Conflicts
    • Some systems have broken when attempting to enter/access data about us!
    • Example: Our family switched driver's insurance since the original company's program broke down when adding children with the same birthdate.
    • Example: Security checks may take longer because having two individuals with the same birthdates is suspicious!
    • Example: The pharmacy's AI teleoperator always asks for a birthdate to identify the child a parent is requesting medication refills for. Unfortunately, it can only identify the birthdate with one twin.
    • Example: Twins' credit scores often get mixed up. (Pro tip: keep your twins' names as different as possible to reduce this likelihood!)
    • Example: Insurance companies charge my family incorrectly for one twin based on the guidelines they set out for age. Their systems seem to break down when calculating the cost for individuals with the same birthdate!
    • Unanswerable Survey Questions
    • Ever feel that survey questions aren't made expecting someone with your experience? This happens for twins, too!
    • Example: Back when we were first applying to colleges, we needed to submit a form stating how many family members would be in college at the same time as us. This would affect the financial aid we would receive. However, the form required we specify when these family memebers started college and wouldn't let us list the same academic year.
